This year, I set a goal for myself to try and play, or replay in a lot of cases, as many Resident Evil games as I could in one year. I got a pretty decent way through the series, and I thought it’d make for a perfect tier list article! Instead of trying to talk about all the games I played, I thought it’d be more interesting (and easier) to focus on the older entries, which I think most people don’t go back to play as much as they do the more recent releases - that includes me, as I hadn’t played most of these games before this year.

Before we get into it, if you’re not familiar with the Resident Evil franchise, you might be wondering what I mean by “classic”. These are the games that have fixed camera angles and tank controls (pressing up moves the character forward, down reverses, and left and right rotates, independent of camera perspective) - which means these are all from before the series went with a third-person shooter perspective in Resident Evil 4. I think the different perspective and controls come across as archaic to a lot of people, but there’s some really good stuff in here, and they’re more accessible than ever thanks to the PC ports becoming available for the PS1 era.

<h2 style="background-color: #bfff7f; text-align: center;">D</h2>

### Resident Evil 0

![RE0]({{ page.contentDir }}/re0.png){:width="720"}

Resident Evil 0 is a pretty rough game all around, and I think it’s my most disliked Resident Evil in the franchise. There are so many things that feel horrendously underbaked and poorly implemented. I would say that Resident Evil games benefit from having iterative improvements from lessons learned from the previous entries, but that is just not the case in 0.

It has some of the worst creature types in the series, like the super-fast monkeys that can pretty much stun-lock you, or toads that spawn at random in certain areas and one-shot kill you. This could probably have been overcome, except when you’re fighting things, you’re stuck with a frustrating partner that could only be set to either “shoot all the time” or “don’t do anything”. It makes the fight or flight combat choices feel less to do with your confidence in dealing with a fight, and more about how much you can trust the dumb AI. The multiple sequences where you’re split apart from your partner are honestly some of the best moments in the game because you don’t have to worry about them.	

Mix the annoying partner system with a very linear feeling map, a story that is noteworthily bad even by RE standards, and the absolutely baffling choice to remove item boxes so resource management instead just becomes leaving things on the floor until you run out of space - and you’re left with an experience so underwhelming that you understand why this was the last “classic style” Resident Evil. I just don’t understand how they messed it up so bad.

<h2 style="background-color: #ffff7f; text-align: center;">C</h2>

### Resident Evil (1996)

![RE1]({{ page.contentDir }}/re1.jpg){:width="720"}

For the first game in a franchise that has evolved so much from simpler beginnings, I’d say the original Resident Evil holds up pretty well all things considered. It’s also the game I have the least to talk about.

It feels stiffer to play than everything else (in particular, I never really found my groove when it came to dodging around enemies), but it does a good to great job at establishing everything else other games would iterate from. I think the map design flows well, at least while you’re playing as Jill. I have yet to complete a run with Chris; maybe I’ll try to get that done one day. 

If you do want to check this out, I would recommend either the GOG PC version or the DS port (emulation or otherwise). The former is an easily accessible version of the original, and the latter has some fun quality of life improvements like quick turn that make it a bit nicer to play. They both still have the iconic dub while [avoiding the infamous fart trumpets.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJYvCHm3Ov4)

<h2 style="background-color: #ffdf7f; text-align: center;">B</h2>

### Resident Evil – Code: Veronica X

![RE CVX]({{ page.contentDir }}/cvx.png){:width="720"}

Code Veronica X (CVX) gets better the further away I get from it. I don’t know if that means it’s actually good or if distance just makes the heart grow fonder. 

There’s a lot that feels wrong when it comes to CVX. Respawning enemies in some areas of the game could be an interesting subversion of the Resident Evil flow, but it’s inconsistently applied - there’s no visual indication that they will respawn, and the areas where they do seem randomly selected - so you’re reluctant to actually engage with zombies out of fear of wasting precious resources, especially in the early game. Steve Burnside is one of the worst characters in any Resident Evil. The pacing and flow of the game is also quite haphazard. I felt very lost in Rockfort Island while playing as Claire, where there were long stretches of exploration where I didn’t find much new and couldn’t figure out if I was on the right path because nothing was happening. But then there are also stretches where very hard boss fights happen pretty much out of nowhere, and if you’re not bringing the right guns or saving enough ammo and healing, you’re in for a very hard time. I have heard about the game having progression soft locks for years, which put me off from playing it, but while playing it, I thought it was pretty mitigated by saving often (and rotating my saves as well).

Despite all that though, I still had a pretty good time with the game. The game’s 3D environments and camera movements are a really cool evolution on the static camera formula the series had up until now. Because of the time it was released, everything has this vague Matrix inspiration to it that makes it feel very early 2000s in a charming way. In a similar fashion, the English dub has some very of the era Canadian dub performances, with some fantastic campy line readings that I can’t link because they’re spoilers. It has probably become my second most quoted Resident Evil game, after 4. Just some all time bangers. I think CVX is going to be a game I will love to replay because I know all the little quirks and traps to look out for, which I think is a very good thing for a Resident Evil game!

<h2 style="background-color: #ffbf7f; text-align: center;">A</h2>

### Resident Evil 3: Nemesis

![RE3]({{ page.contentDir }}/re3.jpg){:width="720"}

Since the remake of Resident Evil 3 released, people have talked about how inferior it was to the original. Up until this year, the only real exposure I had to the original RE3 was watching Two Best Friends Play Let’s Play as it released in 2015. Having now played the original for myself, wow all those people were totally right. RE3 rocks!

There is such a cool level of dynamism throughout the entire game. It has a “Live Selection” system where at points throughout the game, you’re provided with a quick time event style selection  between two choices, and the one you select can impact things like what enemies you fight or the location of certain characters. Even outside the Live Selection stuff, some enemy and item spawns vary on each run. All of this, alongside a campaign that’s pretty quick to blast through, leads to a wonderfully repayable experience. 

![An example of a Live Selection moment in the game]({{ page.contentDir }}/live-selection-example.png)
_Perhaps my favourite Live Selection choice in the entire game. Source: [Evil Resource](https://www.evilresource.com/resident-evil-3-nemesis/guides/gameplay/live-selection-outcomes)_

By far the best thing about this game is the Nemesis. He is an absolute terror and chases you around the map in a way that’s a lot more akin to Mr. X in the Resident Evil 2 Remake. Dealing with him is the perfect embodiment of the fight or flight combat choice. It feels like he can show up wherever, and if you are not ready to fight him, he will kick your ass. But if you are ready and willing to burn through your resources, you can actually take him down (temporarily), and you do get nice rewards like upgraded guns or healing items. I think this is a design philosophy that’s really lacking in a lot of the pursuer-type enemies in the modern Resident Evil games, where you can only run away and trying to engage is a waste of time. 

The game isn’t perfect, and for all the iterative gameplay improvements RE3 makes compared to 1 and 2, there are some that don’t hit. I thought the puzzle design felt weaker overall, and the quick dodge that was added is super inconsistent and sometimes outright worse than just moving. A lot of the story beats it tries to set up via the unlockable epilogues also go completely nowhere in future instalments, although that’s not really the game’s fault. Still, the game is a perfect send off to the PS1 era of Resident Evil.

<h2 style="background-color: #ff7f7f; text-align: center;">S</h2>

### Resident Evil (2002)

![RE 2002]({{ page.contentDir }}/re2002.png){:width="720"}

Resident Evil (2002) is the first remake of a Resident Evil game, hence the nickname REmake, and it was also the first classic style Resident Evil game I played! I think it might also be the one I have played the most, and watched people play the most too. There are two perspectives I have on the game - how it is as a standalone game, and how it is as a remake of Resident Evil (1996). 

As a standalone game, I think it’s absolutely fantastic. The environments are gorgeous; what they were able to do with pre-rendered backgrounds with the Gamecube’s fidelity still holds up spectacularly. I adore the moody, haunted mansion aesthetics of the Spencer Mansion, and the griminess of the Umbrella labs. The actual level design of the Spencer Mansion loops really well onto itself, and finding new routes and shortcuts really activates my neurones in a way that no other entry in the series seems to do. I can close my eyes and visualise every room and path in that mansion even months after my most recent playthrough, that’s how memorable I find it. 

![Old vs New]({{ page.contentDir }}/oldvsnew.png){:height="900"}
_Original backgrounds (left) vs Remake backgrounds (right). Source: [u/max-zilla on Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/residentevil/comments/ns56jb/resident_evil_1996_2002_background_comparisons/)_

As a remake, I think it does a great job of remixing and expanding Resident Evil (1996). It crafts its own visual identity and adds some cool new lore and enemies to make it a worthwhile experience for original fans, while still holding true to the fundamental core experience of the original. The coolest thing it adds are the Crimson Head type enemies. If a zombie is killed but not properly disposed of (i.e. it was not decapitated or had its remains burnt), it has a chance of coming back as a Crimson Head. In the original games, once you kill a zombie, you can stop worrying about it. Adding Crimson Heads into the mix really messes with that muscle memory, and it adds another layer of complexity - Do you spend valuable inventory space bringing around a lighter and kerosene in case you do kill zombies? If you kill a zombie, do you remember where you left it in case you do need to burn it? It’s a really fun complication to the experience that I appreciated a lot more coming back to the game after playing the original PS1 trilogy.

I have so much love for Resident Evil (2002). It’s got such a specific vibe that none of the other classic Resident Evil games have, but still feels like it fits in with them. I would have loved to see where the gameplay could have evolved from here if they continued, but I think it’s the perfect sendoff for the format (I am ignoring Zero). This is the game I tell people to play when they’re interested in trying out the classic style Resident Evil games. It’s on every platform, and it has a modern controls option, which, while inferior to tank controls, makes the game more easily accessible.  

### Resident Evil 2

![RE2]({{ page.contentDir }}/re2.jpg){:width="720"}

I’m not sure what I can say about the original Resident Evil 2 that hasn’t already been talked about for the past 28 years. It’s still the pinnacle for what a survival horror game should be like. 

Dashing through the overrun Raccoon City, and trying to avoid all the zombies as you try to make it to the R.P.D. in the beginning of the game really sets the tone of what to expect - a decidedly more cinematic vibe than its predecessor, with bombastic (at least by PS1 standards) cutscenes and action set pieces. The actual moment-to-moment gameplay feels a lot more tuned around that increased action as well. RE2 is the game where I found it easiest to move around enemies without getting hit; it really made me appreciate tank controls more than I already did. Despite the added action focus though, there’s still some really great puzzles and exploration. RE2 just does it all so well. 

If you’ve only ever played the Resident Evil 2 Remake, I think it’s really worth trying out the original. They both have their own benefits, but if there’s one thing the original does definitively better is the multiple scenarios. In the remake, it feels like an afterthought to some extent. In the original, you’re missing out if you don’t play both A and B scenarios. They are intentionally designed so that the two scenarios work together to paint a clear picture of the story. You don’t even get to see the iconic Mr. X until you do a B scenario!  It’s also great to see what choices you make during your A scenario impact your B scenario thanks to the “Zapping System”. There’s admittedly not a lot of them (~10 or so), but it’s cool every time you notice it happen. 

Resident Evil 2 is just so good. I understand that PS1 graphics and tank controls might not be for everyone, but you’re also missing out on a certified classic if you let that get in your way. 

<hr>

It’s been a lot of fun to play through all these different Resident Evil games this year! The classic games have such a fun through-line of evolution in scale and gameplay that comes from being released so close to each other, and I really think fans of the remakes should go back and play them.
